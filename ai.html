On the slide

A grade scale from the slide itself.

The controls are cut and stained with the tissue, on the same glass and in the same run. Three control lines of known p53 staining go through the stain with the tissue and set that slide's grade scale. If they did not stain enough to grade the tissue, the case reads No call.

Overexpression

Strong staining in nearly every nucleus

Wild-type

Weak to moderate, varying cell to cell

Null

No staining in the nuclei

The three control lines, stained on the same glass as the patient's tissue.

How it works

Four steps, one case.

The lab and the pathologist stay in charge of every step. The software does the counting. Or watch it: one case, start to finish, in an 80-second video.

1

Upload

Add the H&E and the p53 slide. The three control lines are already on the p53 slide.

2

Mark

The lab confirms the three control lines. The pathologist marks the tumor region to score. The software suggests both; a person confirms them.

3

Analyze

Every tumor nucleus inside the region is graded 0 to 3+ against the slide's own controls.

4

Review & sign

A one-page report. The pathologist edits the summary, then signs.

Why it matters

A p53 call depends on who reads the slide.

p53 staining is one of the clearest signals in gynecologic pathology, when it is read the same way every time.

0.61–0.67

is the agreement (Fleiss κ) between readers of mixed experience scoring the same p53 slides. Substantial, yet short of almost perfect.

What PCI adds

3 controls

On every slide, stained with the tissue, so each read is anchored to the same three reference patterns. Then every tumor nucleus is graded against them.

Sources: Hussain, Howard, Syed, Allgäuer, Gong, Xing & Andersen, Int J Gynecol Pathol 2019;38(5):449–458, doi 10.1097/PGP.0000000000000537 (the cell-line p53 control panel); Karathanasis & Andersen, Lab Invest 106 (2026) 104770, doi 10.1016/j.labinv.2025.104770 (USCAP 2026 abstract: the machine-learning read with cell-line controls).
